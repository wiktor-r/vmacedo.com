How much can two inks do?

The brief: a folder for a band's show in Fortaleza, six panels, one photo and one text on each, delivered with every mark a printer needs. I gave it two Pantone inks and let them overprint into a third.

Vansire is a dream-pop duo from Minnesota; the show is imagined at the Dragão do Mar amphitheatre for their fourth album, Taking Solace. Dream pop is hazy, so the photographs became coarse halftones — a 45-line screen you can see with the naked eye — printed in a single blue.

Two inks, three colours

The folder prints in Pantone 2728 C, a strong blue for photographs and information, and Pantone 806 C, a fluorescent pink for accents. Where the pink overprints the blue it makes a third tone without a third plate. Type is held to three styles only: Helvetica Neue Bold, Helvetica Neue Medium and Baskerville Italic.

The constraint is the style: one screen, two inks, three typefaces.

Designed in the order it unfolds

A roll fold has a reading order, and the panels follow it: the cover first, then the first reveal, where panels 2 and 3 are seen together and so were designed as a pair, then the full inside, then the back with how to get there. Every panel holds one photo and one text, as the brief asked.

The closed file carries crop and registration marks, colour bars, fold marks, page information and 3 mm bleed, packaged from Illustrator with its links and exported as PDF/X-4.
