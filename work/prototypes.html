Interaction design · Working prototypes

Twenty prototypes that teach by doing

Outside class I build small apps where a skill is learned through action, not explanation: abacus beads that guide mental arithmetic and then disappear, syntax assembled by fitting pieces, a keyboard map where the symbol performs the move instead of describing it.

Since2025, ongoing
Count20 pieces

Method20 written design principles

Revised from the user's mistakes

Each prototype gets reworked from where people go wrong. The rule that came out of building FormDrill: when the design is wrong, change the structure of the exercise — don't add a hint or a scenario around it.

Those corrections became twenty named principles — Removable Scaffold, Direct Action, Winning Condition, Recall, Hidden Unity and more — each stating a common mistake and its fix. They now decide how every new piece is designed. A shared, documented theme covers the whole family; two pieces leave it on purpose, where depth or a night sky is the subject.

Soroban

Japanese abacus: each operation broken into discrete moves, one per slide. On tens complements the abacus shows why, not just what.

Unparse

Build a sentence's syntax by fitting pieces together instead of drawing a tree. The roguelike systems were built, then cut.

SpatialMath

Power, root and logarithm as one notation where each role has a body: sphere, cone, target.

Tense Machine

One sentence, BOB EATS THE BURGER, rewritten live as you turn tense, aspect and voice.
