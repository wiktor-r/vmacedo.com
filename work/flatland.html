Book design

What does an edition owe its author?

Flatland is an 1884 novella about a square who discovers a third dimension. Making a new edition of it meant 128 pages of decisions — most of them about what to leave exactly as Abbott made it.

Editorial Design, Unifor · 2026 · individual

FormatA5, 128 pages
Signatures8 of 16 pages
TypeEB Garamond 12/15.6 pt
SourceProject Gutenberg #97

The book is set in EB Garamond at 12 on 15.6 points, thirty-two lines to the page, justified, with widows and orphans held to two lines. Parts open on a right-hand page with an epigraph; sections open on a new page, thirty millimetres down. It closes at 128 pages: eight signatures of sixteen.

Keeping Abbott's drawings

The eleven figures are the author's own drawings from the 1884 edition, reproduced at 200 dpi rather than redrawn. Four of them carry Abbott's hand lettering. Redrawing only the other seven would have left two kinds of figure living in one book, so none were redrawn.

Small editorial calls

The 1884 preface, which comes at the end of the Gutenberg text, was moved to the front, where a preface belongs. The author's four notes were not reproduced, and their callouts went with them — said plainly on the credits page. Spelling and punctuation stay as the original.

The layout itself was produced by script in InDesign, from the text to the finished pages, so a change in the rules re-flowed the whole book instead of one page at a time.
