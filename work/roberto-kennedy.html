Roberto Kennedy: visual identity for a photographer

Roberto Kennedy photographs hotels, companies and architecture, and he wanted a brand that felt serious and professional. We designed an RK monogram that frames a point of light.

Visual Identity Systems, Unifor · 2026 · in a pair

ClientRoberto Kennedy, photographer
TeamIn a pair
DeliveredBrand, 6 versions, applications
Defence6 min 35 s, deck + video

A brand for a photographer is a challenging case. His work is full of photographs, and the colour belongs to them, so the brand can hardly use colour of its own. There are also few visual motifs for photography that aren't clichés: most photographers' logos use a camera.

The briefing with Roberto came down to four points: hotel and corporate work; seriousness; structures, because much of what he photographs is façades, columns and interiors; and framing, which for him is the essence of photography. From those came three attributes for the brand, minimalism, precision and professionalism, and one constraint: the brand would have to sit next to his photographs without competing with them.

Exploring

We explored several directions, mostly typographic. We sketched exhaustively, with the book Logo Modernism as a strong reference. We left the camera out and tested elements that weren't clichés, thinking more abstractly about how to convey elegance and professionalism to his clients: hotels, companies and architecture. We explored perspective, vision and calligraphy.

Six directions came out of the first brainstorms: perspectives, flow, a spiral, calligraphy, a block, and a frame with a centre. We chose the one that didn't depict any equipment: an R and a mirrored K between two stems.

Framing

Along the way we decided to fit the R with the K, and the project began to reveal itself: the concept of framing. The outer stems of the R and the mirrored K work as a frame, and the gap between the letters holds a four-pointed star, a point of light. The logo literally frames a subject, light, which is what a photographer works with.

Framing the light.

Each element answers a point of the briefing. The two stems delimit a central point, the way a photographer delimits a scene. They also recall the columns he photographs, and the star between them is the light. The high-contrast serif of the letters carries the elegance that hotels and companies expect from him.

From light to spark

The star comes from photography too. Photographed with a small aperture, a point of light opens into rays, and that four-pointed shape became the star. The presentation video shows it as a sequence: a photo darkens until one point of light is left, the point opens into rays, and the rays close into the star.

From sketch to monogram

The monogram took seven steps to reach its final form. The first was the sketch of R and K between two stems. The second, the first digital version: an R and a mirrored K. Then the K's arm was crossed over the R's bowl; the two letters were made the same width, with the top of the K behind the R; the top of the K was released from the edge; the stems were thickened, the serifs drawn out and the central gap enlarged; and finally the star went into the central gap.

Construction

The monogram is symmetric: the R and the mirrored K sit on either side of a vertical axis. Both stems have the same thickness, a module m, and the whole mark is twelve modules wide. The star sits on the axis, in the space between the letters, 1.6 modules across.

The frame as a visual element

The concept gave us another tool: the frame itself. Without the letters, the two stems become a frame that can crop a photograph. At small sizes the same frame, with the star inside, becomes the brand's symbol.

Versions and small sizes

The brief asked for six versions: primary, secondary, simplified, greyscale, line and negative. The primary sets the monogram over the name; the secondary runs horizontal, for low, wide spaces such as a website header. The simplified version is the monogram alone, down to 24 pixels; below that, the frame symbol is used, legible down to 16. The line version drops the word Fotografia. The negative is white on black or on a dark photo, with the stems and the name slightly thinned to compensate for white on black.

Colour and type

There is no accent colour: the palette is six tones from black to white, so the brand doesn't compete with the photographs. Type is Cormorant, a high-contrast serif in keeping with the monogram, for the logotype and titles, and Jost, a geometric sans serif, for text, captions and credits.

Applications

The identity in use, shown in mockups: a sign in a hotel lobby, the monogram blind-embossed on a print, the cards out in the world, the box the prints are delivered in, a badge and a door sign for shoots, a cap, a luggage tag, a travel notebook and a camera strap. The mockups were generated with AI from the final artwork.
