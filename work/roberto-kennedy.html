Roberto Kennedy: visual identity for a photographer

Roberto Kennedy photographs hotels, companies and architecture, and he wanted a brand that felt serious and professional. We designed an RK monogram that frames a point of light.

Visual Identity Systems, Unifor · 2026 · in a pair

ClientRoberto Kennedy, photographer
TeamIn a pair
DeliveredBrand, 6 versions, applications
Defence6 min 35 s, deck + video

A brand for a photographer is a challenging case. His work is full of photographs, and the colour belongs to them, so the brand can hardly use colour of its own. There are also few visual motifs for photography that aren't clichés: most photographers' logos use a camera.

Exploring

We explored several directions, mostly typographic. We sketched exhaustively, with the book Logo Modernism as a strong reference. We left the camera out and tested elements that weren't clichés, thinking more abstractly about how to convey elegance and professionalism to his clients: hotels, companies and architecture. We explored perspective, vision and calligraphy.

Framing

Along the way we decided to fit the R with the K, and the project began to reveal itself: the concept of framing. The outer stems of the R and the mirrored K work as a frame, and the gap between the letters holds a four-pointed star, a point of light. The logo literally frames a subject, light, which is what a photographer works with.

Framing the light.

The star comes from photography too. Photographed with a small aperture, a point of light opens into rays, and that four-pointed shape became the star. The presentation video shows it as a sequence: a photo darkens until one point of light is left, the point opens into rays, and the rays close into the star.

The frame as a visual element

The concept gave us another tool: the frame itself. Without the letters, the two stems become a frame that can crop a photograph. It is used on the portfolio cover and on some Instagram posts; the rest of the feed is photographs without a frame and text posts on black.

Versions, colour and type

The brief asked for six versions: primary, secondary, simplified, greyscale, line and negative. The simplified version is the monogram alone, down to 24 pixels; below that, the frame symbol is used, legible down to 16. There is no accent colour: the palette is six tones from black to white, so the brand doesn't compete with the photographs. Type is Cormorant for the logotype and titles, Jost for text.

Applications

The identity in use, shown in mockups: a sign in a hotel lobby, the monogram blind-embossed on a print, the box the prints are delivered in, a badge and a door sign for shoots, a travel notebook, a cap, a luggage tag, a camera strap and the cards out in the world. The mockups were generated with AI from the final artwork.
