Why does an NGO lose the donors who aren't companies?

Associação Voar keeps whole programmes alive on a single corporate donor, while the people who give a little every month barely hear from it. We went to find out why, designed an app around the gap, tested it, and wrote it up.

Design Research, Unifor · semester 2025.2 · group project

CourseDesign Research
MethodDouble Diamond
OutputApp prototype · paper
PublishedISSN 2965-9396

Associação Voar works with children, teenagers and their families in vulnerable situations in Fortaleza, offering education, culture, sport and leisure. On our first visit to its headquarters, the staff mentioned, almost in passing, that entire projects depended on a single company's contribution.

That sentence set the question for the semester. If one corporate donor carries a project, what happens to the individual donor — the person who gives twenty reais a month? Nationally the picture was already worrying: the share of Brazilians who donated money fell from 66% in 2015 to 52% in 2022, and trust in NGOs fell with it.

Finding out before designing

We followed the Double Diamond and kept the first half honest: no solution until the problem was understood. The field notes from the visit and a round of desk research on giving in Brazil went into a CSD matrix — what we were certain of, what we supposed, what we doubted.

The doubts became a semi-structured interview script, and we ran three interviews with Voar staff to close them.

Three findings came out of the interviews. Voar received money through a third-party platform that kept a percentage of every donation. It had no income of its own, so everything depended on giving. And there was no ongoing relationship with individual donors at all.

The problem wasn't that people didn't want to give. It was that giving was a one-way door.

From pains to a product

In an affinity session we reduced the interviews to short fragments and grouped them until patterns appeared. Three requirements survived: a simpler sign-up before the first donation, a channel Voar controls so no fraction of each gift is lost, and continuous communication with the people who give.

We benchmarked ShareTheMeal, the UN World Food Programme's app, as the reference for a low-friction donation, and drew four task flows: sign up and donate; follow the association's activities and volunteer openings; buy from a small shop; and the donor's own profile.

Testing it

The low-fidelity prototype went to usability tests with potential users. Their feedback was processed and built into a mid-fidelity digital prototype — the version we delivered.

From project to paper

The project became an experience report, published in the proceedings of the VI Encontro de Curricularização da Extensão at Unifor in 2026. An expanded version is being adapted for IHC 2026, the Brazilian Symposium on Human Factors in Computing Systems.
